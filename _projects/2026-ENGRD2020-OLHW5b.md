# Design Problem

Imagine you have a macadamia nut that you want to crack open by hand using a simple lever nutcracker.



## (a) Mechanical Design and Analysis

Draw a figure of the nut cracker with the nut when it’s about to crack. Calculate the necessary dimensions of the nutcracker and come up with a design to make this task feasible. You can assume a very simple geometry for the nutcracker to make your calculations easier.

You will need to find typical values for the inputs, such as:

- Average size of macadamia nuts

- Construct free body diagram of generic handheld nutcracker

- Derive a relationbship for relevant dimensions $(L_1 & L_2)$ using input parameters and equillibrum equations derived from free body diagrams from previous two steps 

$$
\sum M_A = 0 = F_N L_1 - F_g L_2 \\
F_N L_1 = F_g L_2 \\ 
\frac{F_N}{F_g} = \frac{L_2}{L_1} = \frac{2178 N}{329 N} = 6.62 \\
L_2 = 6.62 L_1 \\
$$

 - Design Choice: Let $L_1 = $ 2.25 the average diameter of a macademia nut 

$$
L_1 = 2.25(1 in) = 2.25 in \\ 
L_2 = 6.62(2.25 in) = 14.9 in \\
$$

## Discussion of Usability

The relative dimensions of the nutcracker I’ve designed is physically reasonable I.e it is reasonable for the overall length of the nutcracker (L2) to be approx. 6x longer then just the head (L1). However, when looking up the average length of a macadamia nutcracker, I found that 10in is more common for overall length (Source: Amazon YsXoWiui Premium Macademia Nut Cracker Set). I believe that while my calculated dimensions are physically reasonable, they are above average (L2 = 14.9 in, nearly 150% the average) because I assumed max grip strength for an adult female which is significantly lower (by approx. 100 N, Source: NIH) then that of an adult male. Because I assumed this value for Fg was lower, a longer lever arm (L2) from pin A was needed to balance the moment generated by Fn. Realistically, I should use the average max grip strength for males because it is better to overestimate from an engineering design perspective.

## Approach
### Part A

- Construct free body diagram of the handle as sinmpliy supported beam model
- Using vertical equilibrium analysis, solve for the vertical reaction at point A
- Construct free body diagram for slice of beam model to reveal internal loads & moment
- Equate internal moment function from previous step to the beam deflection equation:
$$
EIy'' = M(x)
$$
- Integrate the above equation twice and apply boundry condtions to derive the y(x) equation for deflection
- Solve for y'(x) = 0 to determine a realistic point along the beam that experiences maxmium deflection

### Part B

- Choose a material based on known mass effiency specifications
